Answered before you have to ask

Ask Food Commons OS: 10 questions, already answered

The ten questions this workspace hears most from government policymakers, corporate or funder decision-makers, and students and teachers — each answered in full below, grounded in a specific module. Everything on this page works with the file saved to disk and opened with no internet connection at all.

No network call, ever, on this page. These answers were written in advance and shipped inside the file — the same file that promises "no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no hidden network calls" in its footer. Reading them costs nothing, needs nothing online, and sends nothing anywhere.

PolicymakerWon't this just build a new dependency we can't undo later?

That's the right first question, and it's the reason the Capability and dependence assessment module exists as its own module rather than being folded into a general progress score. It asks you to name, in writing, what would break and who would be stranded if this workspace, a specific partner, or a specific funder disappeared tomorrow — and it keeps that answer separate from "documentation steps," so a heavily-documented project can still honestly score as fragile.

Structurally, every project also carries a governance gate whose text is fixed and cannot be edited away: "who can challenge, exit and correct decisions." If a project can't answer that gate, the tool's own review flags it as incomplete — it does not quietly pass.

PolicymakerWhat's a concrete lever we could actually pull this week — not a five-year plan?

The Open the pathways register exists specifically because the blocker is almost never a food shortage — it's a locked loading dock, an unsigned liability waiver, or a paperwork requirement nobody owns. It lists real, currently-active mechanisms with sources: joint-use/shared-use facility agreements, the (US) Good Samaritan Food Donation Act's donor-liability protection, the Community Eligibility Provision for high-poverty schools, and USDA's geographic-preference procurement rule, among others.

Pair that with the Institutional procurement studio, which records a specific buyer, a specific volume, and a review status — so "we should look into local procurement" becomes one page you can hand to a specific office by Friday.

Corporate / funderHow do we support this without it looking — or becoming — a way to buy influence?

The Funding and incentive design laboratory asks for the same fields for every instrument regardless of who's proposing it: the mechanism, the jurisdiction, the target, the incentive theory, and — not optional — the safeguards. A grant or discount that can't state its own safeguard in writing doesn't get filed as ready.

The load-bearing constraint sits in governance: "who can challenge, exit and correct decisions" is asked of every project, funder-backed or not, and the zero-harm note is explicit that nothing here may make food access "contingent on labour, membership, an award score, or any single platform" — including yours.

Corporate / funderHow do we measure real impact instead of just counting activity?

The Evidence and impact laboratory forces a distinction most dashboards blur: an Observed number is not a Documented claim, which is not a Projected estimate, which is not a Hypothesis. A baseline and a follow-up figure are recorded separately, and the tool reports the raw difference labelled explicitly as "not causal impact" — it will not silently upgrade a before/after number into a claim of what caused it.

For comparing options, the Alternatives & economics lab runs transparent, editable arithmetic (annualized cost, cost per edible kilogram) and states its own limits in the same breath: no discounting, and cost-per-kg alone should never be the basis for comparing against school meals or direct assistance.

Student / educatorI'm a student or a teacher — what's a real project I can start, not just a worksheet?

Start in the Entity & role guide and pick "Community / household / neighbourhood group" (or whichever role fits) to filter the whole tool down to what's relevant right now, then open Project studio and record one real, small, local initiative — a school garden, a shared tool library, a mapped surplus-donation route — as an actual project with actual gates, not a hypothetical. The Knowledge library holds ready patterns to adapt rather than invent cold.

"Safe first actions," in the Overview, is deliberately the smallest useful step: map one asset, log one partner conversation, or read the seven non-negotiable questions before writing a single form field.

Student / educatorHow do we make sure students are genuine participants, not props for a photo-op?

The seven non-negotiable questions in "Start here" open with "Who identified the need?" and "Who has access regardless of income or ability?" — both written to be asked before a project is scored as documented, not after. The zero-harm note is explicit that nothing here may be used to "compel labour" or make participation a condition of access.

Practically: never enter identifying information about a specific child into this file — the Child nutrition module is built for aggregate counts only, and the "Start here" module carries a standing warning against uploading sensitive household or student data into any exported package, since this browser file has no encryption or access control.

EveryoneWhat's the single most common reason efforts like this fail — and does the tool actually help with it?

Almost never a shortage of food; almost always a locked gate — a facility nobody's signed use of, a fear of liability, a form that never gets filled in, or a single supplier/partner/permit that everything else quietly depends on. That's why Open the pathways exists as a first-class module rather than a footnote, and why Risks, redundancy & stress testing asks every project to name its shared dependencies and a backup owner before calling anything resilient.

The Capability and dependence assessment adds the harder-to-face version of the same question: even a fully "documented" project can still be one supplier or one grant away from collapse, and the tool is built to let that show rather than average it away.

PolicymakerWhat about the kids these programs always seem to miss — summer, holidays, or families who don't know the paperwork exists?

Those specific, named gaps are exactly what the Child nutrition & access gap module records, and the Research library keeps live, dated, sourced examples of programs built for precisely this problem: USDA's Summer EBT (SUN Bucks) for the summer gap, the UK's Holiday Activities and Food programme for school breaks, USDA's Child and Adult Care Food Program for the children CACFP reaches outside standard K-12 hours, USDA's WIC vendor-access rule for "benefits exist but nowhere nearby accepts them," and Brazil's PNAE procurement mandate as a structurally different model worth knowing exists.

The Open the pathways register tags each lever by category — eligibility paperwork, facility access, procurement — so a seasonal or paperwork gap can be logged against a documented real-world fix rather than left as a vague complaint.

Corporate / funderWhat data do you collect about us or the people we serve, and where does it go?

Nowhere, by default. The Data, provenance & privacy module and the footer on every single view both say the same thing: no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no hidden network calls. The only place this file ever sends anything is a file you choose to export from that same module — it is not uploaded, synced or phoned home automatically.

The "Methods & sources" module lists, in its own words, what a real deployment still has to do independently before this counts as safe for institutional use — including a cybersecurity and accessibility audit — because a local-only file is a planning aid, not a certified system of record.

EveryoneWe have maybe 90 days and a handful of people. What's the realistic first move?

Resist the urge to fill in every module at once. The 90-day pilot launcher exists so one project can move through a fixed milestone checklist and a short set of acceptance tests instead of an open-ended plan. A workable first pass: one project record, one asset or partner logged, and one of the eight gates ("community need, participation & consent" is usually the honest starting point) actually documented before touching the rest.

"Safe first actions" on the Overview lists the smallest real steps on purpose — reading the seven non-negotiable questions counts as progress on day one.

Five complementary outcomes across six scales

Capacity: produce and preserve where viable. Distributed ownership: permit independent growers, co-ops and institutions. Reciprocity: exchange on transparent terms. Redundancy: test alternatives instead of counting suppliers. Ecological durability: protect water, soil, biodiversity and labour conditions.

Household → neighbourhood → city-region → province/state → country → international partners. This is an interoperability map, not a chain of command; Indigenous governments and other rights-holders retain distinct legal authority.

Agreement or decision record

Compacts and decisions

Seven clauses for an intercooperative compact

1. Production, capacities and seasons

State capabilities, limits, reliability and the conditions under which advertised capacity may change.

2. Exchange and compensation

Set pricing, currencies, terms, producer viability, payment timing, quality and transport responsibility.

3. Infrastructure ownership

Specify title, maintenance, asset access, depreciation, insurance and capital contributions.

4. Resilience and emergencies

Preserve minimum needs of each community; specify emergency support thresholds, compensation and alternatives.

5. Representation and disputes

Define who votes, conflicts of interest, appeal paths, accessible decision-making and independent review.

6. Equitable access

Prevent income, disability or compulsory unpaid labour from becoming barriers to essential food access.

7. Ecology, privacy and withdrawal

Respect water and soil limits; minimize data; define consent, termination and distribution of shared assets.

Measurement record

Evidence ledger

Measurement protocol

  1. Define the outcome and population before starting.
  2. Capture a credible baseline with units and timing.
  3. Choose an appropriate comparison where feasible and ethical.
  4. Document confounders, attrition, uncertainty and exclusions.
  5. Report null and negative results alongside positive ones.
  6. Never equate harvested kilograms with food security or dietary adequacy.
The FAO Food Insecurity Experience Scale is a validated survey methodology requiring its proper questions, sampling and statistical analysis. This generic metric form does not calculate FIES prevalence or certification.
Compare, don't presume

Alternatives & economics lab

Transparent arithmetic for hypothetical intervention options. Costs are not outcomes; cross-option comparisons require consistent boundaries and comparable units.

Intervention comparison

Enter your own estimates. No values are populated as empirical facts. All calculation is local and ephemeral; use an evidence record to document measured observations.

Results (illustrative arithmetic)

Add a scenario to compare lifecycle annualization and output.
Model: annualized cost = capital ÷ assumed useful years + yearly operating costs. Cost per edible kg = annualized cost ÷ annual safe edible kilograms where output is positive. Funding gap = annualized cost − committed funding, floored at zero. The ±20% range shown is a fixed illustrative sensitivity check (cost up / output down vs. cost down / output up), not a statistically derived confidence interval. This simplified model excludes discounting and whatever you omit from inputs.

Do not compare assistance or school meals on cost per kg alone; nutrition, affordability, access and causal impact require additional measures. This module does not select a preferred policy.

From idea to field evidence

90-day pilot launcher

The calendar organizes decisions, not agricultural maturation. Crop yields, approvals and real outcomes may take seasons or years.

Days 1–15 · Establish the baseline

Map needs, available capacity, suitable land, access barriers, existing services and interested participants. Deliver stakeholder register and baseline protocol.

Days 16–30 · Establish authority

Obtain applicable permissions and consent; define governance, legal responsibilities, budget, safety review and opt-out. Do not treat a checklist as approval.

Days 31–45 · Test one exchange

Complete a small voluntary supply or procurement transaction; measure costs, logistics, payment timing and losses.

Days 46–60 · Add one justified asset

Target an identified bottleneck: propagation, storage, equipment, transport, growing space or another evidence-backed constraint.

Days 61–75 · Stress-test

Simulate the loss of a route, supplier or shared utility. Check actual backup capacity and responsibilities.

Days 76–90 · Evaluate and decide

Compare documented results with alternatives; publish consented, privacy-preserving lessons, including failure and unintended effects.

Trucks already arrive · The roadblock is often administrative

Open the pathways: get existing food to children

Most cities are already supplied by logistics networks that work. What keeps a truckload of food from reaching a hungry child is rarely a supply problem — it is a locked loading dock, an unsigned facility agreement, a paperwork threshold, a single-supplier contract, or an official unsure whether acting first is safe for their career. Log the specific roadblock in front of you, then check whether an already-existing, already-used lever applies.

Why "decision-safe" matters: officials often hesitate even once a lever exists, because acting first feels personally risky. Pointing to a law already on the books, or a model agreement other institutions already use, is usually what turns hesitation into action — inventing a new argument rarely is.

Unlock existing capacity before building unnecessary scarcity

Food systems fail children not only when food is absent, but when authority, paperwork, procurement, liability, scheduling, transport or decision-risk prevents existing capacity from reaching them. How to read this system

Observed
An event or number recorded with its method and source; not automatically independently verified.
Documented
A user links evidence or records a review; this app does not verify the author or document.
Projected
An estimated future output or cost under specified assumptions.
Hypothesis
A falsifiable explanation of how an intervention might change an outcome.
Metaphor
Language that supports understanding, not evidence of common causation.

⚖ Zero-Harm & Anti-Inversion

This workspace is for coordinating voluntary, reciprocal food access — never for gatekeeping food, compelling labour, concentrating control over land or distribution, or surveilling the people it serves. Any adaptation of this file that could be used to coerce, exclude or monitor someone is a misuse of the design, not a feature of it: real authority over land, safety and food distribution stays with communities, producers and the applicable regulators. Nothing here makes food access contingent on labour, membership, an award score, or any single platform. No accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no hidden network calls: the only place this file ever sends your data is a file you choose to export. Local autonomy, shared evidence, reciprocal capacity — and the freedom to walk away with your records at any time.
