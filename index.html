Operating doctrine

Start here: independence through cooperation

The operating system coordinates information. It is not a government, regulator, financial service, food retailer, or substitute for consent.

What this operating system does

Build one traceable record for each initiative: the need, scope, land permissions, expert advice, water and ecology, costs, food safety, governance, harvest and distribution, and measured outcomes. Link the project to partners, productive assets, exchange opportunities and risks.

Seven non-negotiable questions

  1. Who identified the need?
  2. Who has authority and consent?
  3. Who bears costs and risks?
  4. Who has access regardless of income or ability?
  5. Who validates safety?
  6. What is the credible comparison?
  7. Who can challenge, exit and correct decisions?

Compacts and decisions

Seven clauses for an intercooperative compact

1. Production, capacities and seasons

State capabilities, limits, reliability and the conditions under which advertised capacity may change.

2. Exchange and compensation

Set pricing, currencies, terms, producer viability, payment timing, quality and transport responsibility.

3. Infrastructure ownership

Specify title, maintenance, asset access, depreciation, insurance and capital contributions.

4. Resilience and emergencies

Preserve minimum needs of each community; specify emergency support thresholds, compensation and alternatives.

5. Representation and disputes

Define who votes, conflicts of interest, appeal paths, accessible decision-making and independent review.

6. Equitable access

Prevent income, disability or compulsory unpaid labour from becoming barriers to essential food access.

7. Ecology, privacy and withdrawal

Respect water and soil limits; minimize data; define consent, termination and distribution of shared assets.

Conflicts of interest and complaints

Clause 5 asks who handles conflicts of interest and appeals. Record them in the integrity registers — by role, never by name — so each decision shows who stepped back and how concerns were answered.

Fair process without surveillance

Integrity: conflicts of interest & complaints

Two registers that let a governance body show it handles conflicts of interest and complaints fairly — without this file ever holding anyone's name, contact details or identifying story. It records roles, categories, dates and outcomes. Identities stay with the people themselves, or with a designated role outside this app.

Six design rules

  1. Roles, not names. “Procurement committee seat 2”, never a person.
  2. No contact details. These two forms refuse email addresses and phone numbers.
  3. Food is never conditional. Raising a concern, or being named in one, never changes anyone's access to food.
  4. No retaliation. Retaliation is itself a reportable concern, with its own category.
  5. Small numbers stay hidden. Public summaries hide small counts so no one can be singled out.
  6. Nothing kept forever. Each case carries a review-or-delete date.

Case register

Not an emergency or whistleblowing channel. If someone is in danger, contact emergency services. Serious wrongdoing, abuse, or anything involving a child's safety belongs with the appropriate authority immediately — not in a local planning file. This register helps a community body track ordinary concerns and show that it responds fairly.

Public summary — counts only

Generates plain text for an annual report or a public meeting. It contains counts only: no titles, summaries, roles or dates of individual cases. Any count from 1 up to the threshold is shown as “fewer than” the threshold, and a second count is hidden whenever one hidden value could otherwise be worked out from the total.

Minimum 5. Use a higher number in a small community.

How to run this well

Declare early, decide cleanly

Ask for declarations at the start of every meeting that decides money, suppliers or access. A recusal recorded before a vote protects both the person and the decision.

Acknowledge quickly

Set a response date when a concern arrives, and keep it. People judge fairness by whether they were heard and kept informed, not only by the outcome.

Repair rather than punish

Most concerns are settled by explaining, changing a process or putting something right. A restorative conversation is offered, never required.

Keep handling independent

No one handles a concern about their own role, or decides a matter on which they hold an open declaration.

Report patterns publicly

Publish counts by category and how long responses took, with small numbers hidden. Patterns show where a process is failing people.

Let records go

When a case reaches its review date, check whether the record is still needed for an appeal or a report. If not, delete it.

Measurement protocol

  1. Define the outcome and population before starting.
  2. Capture a credible baseline with units and timing.
  3. Choose an appropriate comparison where feasible and ethical.
  4. Document confounders, attrition, uncertainty and exclusions.

Results (illustrative arithmetic)

Add a scenario to compare lifecycle annualization and output.
Model: annualized cost = capital ÷ assumed useful years + yearly operating costs. Cost per edible kg = annualized cost ÷ annual safe edible kilograms where output is positive. Funding gap = annualized cost − committed funding, floored at zero. The ±20% range shown is a fixed illustrative sensitivity check (cost up / output down vs. cost down / output up), not a statistically derived confidence interval. This simplified model excludes discounting and whatever you omit from inputs.

Do not compare assistance or school meals on cost per kg alone; nutrition, affordability, access and causal impact require additional measures. This module does not select a preferred policy.

From idea to field evidence

90-day pilot launcher

The calendar organizes decisions, not agricultural maturation. Crop yields, approvals and real outcomes may take seasons or years.

Days 1–15 · Establish the baseline

Map needs, available capacity, suitable land, access barriers, existing services and interested participants. Deliver stakeholder register and baseline protocol.

Days 16–30 · Establish authority

Obtain applicable permissions and consent; define governance, legal responsibilities, budget, safety review and opt-out. Do not treat a checklist as approval.

Days 31–45 · Test one exchange

Complete a small voluntary supply or procurement transaction; measure costs, logistics, payment timing and losses.

Days 46–60 · Add one justified asset

Target an identified bottleneck: propagation, storage, equipment, transport, growing space or another evidence-backed constraint.

Days 61–75 · Stress-test

Simulate the loss of a route, supplier or shared utility. Check actual backup capacity and responsibilities.

Days 76–90 · Evaluate and decide

Compare documented results with alternatives; publish consented, privacy-preserving lessons, including failure and unintended effects.

Trucks already arrive · The gate is administrative

Unlock the gates: get existing food to children

Most cities are already supplied by logistics networks that work. What keeps a truckload of food from reaching a hungry child is rarely a supply problem — it is a locked loading dock, an unsigned facility agreement, a paperwork threshold, a single-supplier contract, or an official unsure whether acting first is safe for their career. Log the specific barrier in front of you, then check whether an already-existing, already-used lever applies.

Why "decision-safe" matters: officials often hesitate even once a lever exists, because acting first feels personally risky. Pointing to a law already on the books, or a model agreement other institutions already use, is usually what turns hesitation into action — inventing a new argument rarely is.

Translation into testable features

Affordability: compare cost of adequate diets, not just kilograms produced.

Government demand: procurement opportunities, supplier readiness and real compliance gates.

Infrastructure: measured utilization, maintenance and causal limitations.

Food loss: stage-specific measurements rather than assumed savings.

Schools: meal access and farmer payments separately measured.

Regulatory certainty: dated sources and jurisdiction-specific review; no invented eligibility.

Access barriers: a named gatekeeper and a citable lever per logged barrier — not a general call for "political will."

A route is more than a distance

Route & delivery feasibility

Check whether a specific route can actually deliver in time — and, if cold chain matters, whether it can deliver safe. Add more than one route to compare cost against feasibility side by side. Local and ephemeral, like the scenario lab: nothing here is saved.

Comparison

Add a route to check feasibility and cost.
Model: total cost = cost/kg × quantity. A route is infeasible if transit time exceeds the required delivery window, or — when cold chain is required — exceeds the stated maximum safe transit time. This does not account for traffic variability, breakdowns, customs, or anything you left out of the inputs; it flags what the numbers you entered actually imply.
Epistemic clarity

Methodology, safeguards & references

A coordination tool can improve visibility and consistency. It cannot replace legal authority, qualified practice or valid empirical evaluation.

How to read this system

Observed
An event or number recorded with its method and source; not automatically independently verified.
Documented
A user links evidence or records a review; this app does not verify the author or document.
Projected
An estimated future output or cost under specified assumptions.
Hypothesis
A falsifiable explanation of how an intervention might change an outcome.
Metaphor
Language that supports understanding, not evidence of common causation.

⚖ Zero Harm & Anti-Coercion

Nothing here makes food access contingent on labour, membership, an award score, or any single platform. No accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no hidden network calls: the only place this file ever sends your data is a file you choose to export, and the page itself blocks network requests. Raising a concern can never cost anyone their access to food. Local autonomy, shared evidence, reciprocal capacity — and the freedom to walk away with your records at any time.
